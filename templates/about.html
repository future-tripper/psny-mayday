M'aider

French for "help me"—the origin of our word mayday, the international distress signal.

In these uncertain times, the Poetry Society of New York invites you to answer the call: come help me, help us, help each other through the power of shared words.

The Crown of Sonnets

A centuries-old poetic form where multiple sonnets are linked together. Traditionally, the last line of one sonnet becomes the first line of the next, creating an unbroken chain. In Mayday, we've reimagined this form for the digital age.

How It Works

Starting with classic sonnets from PSNY's collection, pairs of poets write between two consecutive lines of an existing poem. Together, you add 12 new lines—6 lines each, alternating—to create a complete 14-line sonnet.

Your ending line becomes the beginning for another pair, and their ending line seeds yet another collaboration. The result is a living, growing web of interconnected poetry—voices from around the world woven together in a tapestry of mutual aid and beauty.

The Poetry Society of New York

Founded in 1910, PSNY is one of the oldest poetry organizations in the United States. We believe poetry is never a lonely art form. Through readings, workshops, publications, and digital experiments like Mayday, we connect poets and poetry lovers across generations and geographies.
